Mathematics of evaluation

Let yᵢ denote actual full-bloom day of year and ŷᵢ the point prediction, with error eᵢ = ŷᵢ − yᵢ. Positive error means the prediction is late.

Reported summary metrics are unweighted means of the five fold-level metrics, following the shared cv_report() convention. This differs from computing pooled MAE/RMSE/R² over all 575 observations; pooled and fold-mean results should not be interchanged.

04 / Baseline two

Adapted 600°C temperature rule

The traditional 600°C heuristic accumulates daily maximum temperature from February 1 to estimate first flowering. This project targets full bloom, so the temperature threshold is recalibrated from historical full-bloom observations.

Step A · Historical temperature accumulation

For each eligible training station-year, sum the observed daily maximum temperatures through the actual full-bloom date. Only complete February 1-to-full-bloom sequences are eligible for calibration.

07 / Interpretation & caution

Assumptions, limitations and leakage safeguards

What was enforced in the forecasting code

  • Five expanding-window folds and shared station-year evaluation sample.
  • Training-fold-only calibration of temperature thresholds and daily temperature climatologies.
  • February 1 uses no realized February weather; March 1 uses realized February weather only.
  • Observed weather is used strictly before the issue date.
  • 2015–2025 is reserved as the final held-out test period.

Known limitations

1
Historical weather is not a seasonal forecastFuture temperatures are replaced with historical daily averages. Unusual spring warmth or cold spells cannot be anticipated.
2
Geographic aggregation remains coarseEven eight regional thresholds may hide station-level microclimates, elevation differences and coastal effects.
3
Long-term change and temporal instabilityClimatology assumes recent historical bloom timing remains informative. Fold 5 shows particularly high error.
4
Target and predictor availabilityThe standardized feature names align with forecast cutoffs, but upstream imputation and feature construction were not independently audited end-to-end.
5
Calendar and accumulation edge casesThe code uses a February 28 climatological fallback for February 29. The leap-year convention and exact treatment of incomplete sequences should be verified before deployment.
6
Validation selection is not independent testingNational and regional configurations were compared on the same validation years used for model selection. Final generalization remains unmeasured.
Data-quality example — Onahama 1977: The weather file showed 35 missing Tmax values over February–June, but the February 1–April 14 interval through observed full bloom contained all 73 expected days with no missing Tmax. Therefore, the station-year remained eligible for historical full-bloom threshold calibration. This does not imply all subsequent weather records are complete.

Interpretation of R²

A relatively high pooled or fold-level R² can partly reflect substantial geographic differences in typical bloom dates across Japan. It should not be interpreted as evidence that the model reliably predicts each station's year-to-year bloom anomaly.

What remains open

Not yet independently verified: full upstream weather-imputation cutoff safety; leap-day and missing-data edge cases; deployment readiness. The 2015–2025 held-out test evaluation is complete and reported above.

Generalization from validation to held-out testing

The validation-fold mean MAE for 30-year climatology was 3.804 days, versus 5.040 days on the test sample. The regional rule rose from 4.287 to 5.915 days for February 1 and 4.383 to 5.555 days for March 1. This indicates weaker performance on the later test years, but note that the validation figures average five fold-level MAEs equally whereas the test figure is one pooled 2015–2025 MAE. These are informative comparisons, not identical estimands.

Regional calibration continued to outperform the national temperature rule on both test issue dates: MAE was 5.915 vs 7.339 days for February 1 and 5.555 vs 7.157 days for March 1. Unlike the validation result, the regional March 1 model now outperformed regional February 1 on both MAE and hit rate. These observations should be interpreted as results, not as grounds for post-test retuning.

08 / Recommendations

Conclusions and team handover

Recommended benchmark set

Primary historical benchmark: 30-year station climatology, selected because it has the lowest validation MAE (3.804 days). Preferred temperature benchmark: regionally calibrated accumulated-temperature rule, selected because it improves on national calibration for both forecast dates. Reference: retain the national threshold version to quantify the value of regional calibration.

For validation, 30-year climatology had the lowest average error while regional February 1 had the highest ±3-day hit rate. In the independent test, climatology still had the lowest MAE, while regional March 1 achieved the highest hit rate. The team should compare its chosen champion models with these frozen baseline test results on the same eligible 2015–2025 observations.

Appendix / Reproducibility

Methodological record

Evaluation conventions

MAE and RMSE are expressed in days; hit rate is stored as a proportion internally and displayed as a percentage. The table uses equal weighting across the five folds, even though folds have different numbers of observations (148, 127, 106, 98, 96). The R² reported is the arithmetic mean of fold R² values, not pooled R².

Calibration and forecasting decisions

Temperature accumulation starts February 1 and uses daily maximum temperature without a base-temperature adjustment. The full-bloom threshold is the training-set median of accumulation at observed full bloom, calculated nationally or by region. Future temperatures are estimated from station-by-calendar-day historical averages over 30 training-permitted years, with a national fallback. The temperature-rule model is an adapted empirical heuristic, not a physical phenology simulator.

Source materials and provenance

This report consolidates the user's executed Sakura Analytics notebooks and the numerical results shared during the modelling workflow, alongside the team's model development meeting summary and project proposal. Code modules referenced include src/model_data.py and the prepared data/model/ CSVs. The historical 600°C rule was introduced as a traditional flowering heuristic; its adaptation to full bloom is project-specific.

Recommended next project step

The frozen baselines have now been fitted on 1964–2014 and evaluated once on 2015–2025. The next project step is to evaluate the team-selected champion models on the same test observations and compare all results without retuning on the held-out years.
